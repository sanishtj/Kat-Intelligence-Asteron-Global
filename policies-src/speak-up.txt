TITLE: Speak-Up and Whistleblowing Policy
SHORT: speak-up-and-whistleblowing
VERSION: Version 3.1, effective 1 March 2026
OWNER: Ethics and Compliance Office
INTRO: Asteron Global depends on people raising concerns early: about fraud, safety, harassment, corruption, misuse of data or any other wrongdoing. This policy explains the channels available, the protection you will receive, and how concerns are handled. Raising a concern in good faith will never count against you.

## 1. Purpose
This policy encourages and protects the reporting of suspected wrongdoing connected to Asteron Global. It supports the Code of Conduct and Ethics and works alongside the reporting routes in individual policies. Where local law gives stronger protection to reporters, that law applies.

## 2. What can be reported
You can report any genuine concern about conduct connected to the company, including:
- Fraud, bribery, corruption or theft.
- Harassment, bullying or discrimination.
- Risks to health, safety or the environment.
- Misuse of customer or employee data, or other legal or regulatory breaches.
- Falsifying records, accounts or reports.
- Retaliation against someone who has raised a concern.
You do not need to be certain that wrongdoing has occurred, and you do not need evidence in hand. A reasonable concern, honestly held, is enough.

## 3. Who can report
Any employee, intern, contractor, agency worker, supplier or other person who interacts with Asteron Global may use these channels. Concerns about a person are welcome from anyone affected by or aware of the conduct, including witnesses.

## 4. Reporting channels
You may raise a concern through any of these routes:
- Your line manager, or any manager you trust, if you are comfortable doing so.
- The Ethics and Compliance Office, directly and in confidence.
- The Speak-Up channel, an independent reporting service available at all times, by web or phone, in the main languages of the countries where we operate.
- People and Culture, for concerns about harassment or workplace behaviour.
If your concern involves your own manager, use a different route; no one is ever required to report to the person their concern is about.

## 5. Anonymous reporting
You may report anonymously through the Speak-Up channel. Anonymous reports are assessed and investigated on the same basis as named reports. Providing your name, in confidence, usually helps investigators ask follow-up questions and protect you more effectively, but the choice is yours and will be respected either way. Attempts to identify an anonymous reporter are prohibited.

## 6. Confidentiality
Your identity and the details of your concern are shared only with the small number of people who need them to assess, investigate or decide the matter. Records are stored securely with access limited by role. Breaching the confidentiality of a report is itself a disciplinary matter.

## 7. Protection from retaliation
Asteron Global prohibits retaliation of any kind against a person who raises a concern in good faith or helps an investigation. Retaliation includes dismissal, demotion, loss of shifts or duties, exclusion, threats and negative treatment of any sort connected to the report. If you experience or witness retaliation, report it immediately through any channel in section 4; it will be investigated as a separate and serious matter, and substantiated retaliation may result in dismissal of the person responsible.

## 8. How concerns are handled
Concerns are acknowledged within three business days where contact details are known. Each concern is assessed, and where needed investigated by a person with no involvement in the matter. Investigations aim to establish facts fairly, hearing from everyone involved. The reporter is told, in appropriate terms, when the matter is closed. Timelines vary with complexity, but an unexplained silence is never the intended outcome; you may ask for a status update through the channel you used.

## 9. Concerns about senior leaders
Concerns involving a member of the executive team are reported directly to the Chair of the Audit Committee through the Speak-Up channel and are investigated independently of management. No executive may access, direct or close a concern that concerns themselves or their function.

## 10. Bad-faith reports
Reports made in good faith are protected even if they turn out to be mistaken. Knowingly making a false report, or fabricating evidence, is a violation of the Code of Conduct and Ethics and may result in disciplinary action. This section is applied sparingly and never used to discourage genuine concerns.

## 11. Review
The Ethics and Compliance Office reports annually to the Audit Committee on the volume, types and outcomes of concerns, in aggregate and without identifying reporters, and this policy is reviewed against that experience each year.

## 12. Worked examples
Example. An accounts assistant notices regular payments to a supplier that nobody in the team can identify and for which no contract can be found. She has no proof of fraud, and section 2 confirms she needs none: a reasonable concern, honestly held, is enough. She reports it through the Speak-Up channel and may do so anonymously under section 5.
Example. Two weeks after raising a safety concern, an operator finds his overtime shifts have been removed and he has been moved to less desirable duties, with no explanation. A change in treatment connected to a report may be retaliation under section 7. He should report it through any channel in section 4, and it will be investigated as a separate and serious matter.
Example. A finance manager has a concern about the conduct of the Chief Financial Officer and does not trust internal routes. Section 9 provides the answer: concerns involving a member of the executive team go directly to the Chair of the Audit Committee through the Speak-Up channel and are investigated independently of management.
