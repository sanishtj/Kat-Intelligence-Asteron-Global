TITLE: Social Media and External Communications Policy
SHORT: social-media-and-external-communications
VERSION: Version 1.6, effective 5 January 2026
OWNER: Corporate Communications
INTRO: What employees publish online can affect Asteron Global's reputation, customers and legal position within minutes. This policy explains who may speak for the company, what must never be shared, and how personal accounts may be used responsibly.

## 1. Purpose
This policy governs public communications about Asteron Global, including social media, blogs, forums, podcasts, press and public events. It protects confidential information and ensures that statements made in the company's name are accurate and authorised.

## 2. Who may speak for the company
Only employees authorised by Corporate Communications may make statements on behalf of Asteron Global, respond to media enquiries, or publish on company accounts. If a journalist, analyst or member of the public asks you about the company, refer them to Corporate Communications and do not comment, even informally and even if you believe the answer is already public.

## 3. Confidential information
You must never publish, on any platform:
- Customer, supplier or partner information, including names, contracts, pricing or photographs of their premises or documents.
- Unpublished financial results, forecasts, product plans or internal announcements.
- Photographs or screenshots of internal systems, dashboards, documents or whiteboards.
- Personal data about colleagues or customers, in line with the Data Protection and Privacy Policy.
If you are unsure whether something is public, treat it as confidential and ask Corporate Communications before posting.

## 4. Personal accounts
You are free to use social media in a personal capacity, and you may state where you work. When you post about work-related topics, make clear that the views are your own, do not use company logos or titles in a way that suggests you speak for Asteron Global, and never post during a live incident, negotiation or dispute involving the company. Posts that harass or discriminate against colleagues breach the Workplace Harassment Prevention Policy whether they are made in or outside working hours.

## 5. Photographs and recording at work
Do not photograph or record in restricted areas, production zones, laboratories or customer sites, and do not share images showing screens, documents or security arrangements. Site-specific rules displayed at entrances prevail where they are stricter than this policy.

## 6. Crises and incidents
During an incident affecting safety, customers or operations, only the designated crisis team may publish information about it. Do not post updates, photographs or speculation about an ongoing incident, even to correct rumours; refer all enquiries to Corporate Communications, which will issue verified information.

## 7. Endorsements and personal benefit
Do not endorse products or services in a way that trades on your Asteron Global role without disclosing the relationship, and do not accept payment or gifts for posts connected to your role except as permitted by the Anti-Bribery and Corruption Policy.

## 8. Mistakes
If you publish something in error, do not quietly delete it and say nothing: contact Corporate Communications promptly so the record can be corrected openly. Screenshots survive deletion, and an honest correction protects both you and the company.

## 9. Consequences
Breaches of this policy, particularly disclosure of confidential or customer information, may result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal, and legal action where the law has been broken. Good-faith personal expression that does not breach sections 3 to 6 is not restricted by this policy.

## 10. Worked examples
Example. An employee photographs a whiteboard showing next quarter's unannounced product plans and posts it with a comment about exciting times ahead. Unpublished product plans are confidential under section 3, and the photograph must not be posted. If it has already gone up, the employee should contact Corporate Communications promptly under section 8 rather than quietly deleting it.
Example. After an incident at a plant, a journalist messages an employee directly on a social platform asking what happened. Under sections 2 and 6, the employee must not comment, even to correct a wrong detail, and should refer the journalist to Corporate Communications, which will issue verified information.
Example. An employee writes a personal post about trends in her profession, makes clear the views are her own, and uses no company information, logos or customer details. This is permitted under section 4: personal expression that respects the confidentiality rules is not restricted by this policy.
